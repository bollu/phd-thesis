\epigraph{Civilization advances by extending the number of important operations which we can perform without thinking about them}{Alfred North Whitehead, An Introduction to Mathematics}

\lettrine{A}{production-grade} optimizing compiler is a large program.
In LLVM, the peephole rewriter alone is larger than the entire vectorizer!
This thesis asks how such a compiler can be verified.
We cannot verify all of it, so we focus on the peephole rewriter,
whose unit of work is a single, local transformation on a finite window of instructions.
Each transformation is small enough to prove correct,
but the rewriter as a whole accumulates thousands of such proof obligations.

Can we prove them by hand? In principle, yes; in practice it is deathly tedious and error-prone,
as I discovered when I set out to verify an SSA-based peephole rewriter for LLVM's bitvector optimizations.
I first formalized a core calculus for SSA-based compiler intermediate representations,
generic over the user's IR, and proved a generic peephole rewriter correct,
with case studies ranging from fully homomorphic encryption to LLVM's bitvector rewrites.
It is the last of these where the real difficulty began.
The structural half of manipulating SSA came for free; the arithmetic half did not.
The obligations were subtle, and proving them by hand was slow enough that
the thousands of rewrites a production compiler relies on were plainly out of reach.
It is this tedium that drives the demand for push-button automation.

What style of decision procedure should we bet on? I bet on bitblasting:
encode a rich theory into propositional logic, and hand the result to a SAT solver.
The problems I cared about were finite, and were about bit-level representations,
so the fit seemed natural; only later did I appreciate that this same tradition
powers today's fastest SMT solvers.
I helped implement \bvdecide{}, Lean's fixed-width bitblaster,
which carries a bitvector goal all the way down to a SAT instance, solves it,
and has the resulting certificate rechecked by Lean's kernel.
Speed and trust at the same time.
This infrastructure is the bedrock of the rest of the thesis.

But the obligations a compiler hands us are not bitblastable as they stand.
A rewrite has to be correct at \emph{every} register width, whereas a bitblaster wants one fixed width,
and the standard remedy is to enumerate the widths and bitblast each one separately.
We show that this is unnecessary.
Predicates that must hold at all widths reduce to reachability in a finite automaton,
which we decide by $k$-induction and language emptiness, bottoming out, as ever, in SAT.
Next, by a neat encoding trick, widths as bitmasks,
any formula over $n$ symbolic widths reduces to an equisatisfiable single-width one.
This trades an exponential number of solver queries for a \emph{single} query,
and yields a new sound and complete fragment of the multi-width theory.

Floating point was the next missing piece, and the hardest to encode:
its semantics live over the real numbers, which admit no bit-level encoding of their own.
We mechanize the SMT-LIB \qffp{} theory and build verified bitblasting circuits for almost all of its operations,
trustworthy at every combination of exponent and mantissa width.
Doing so left me unconvinced that bitblasting the IEEE semantics directly is the right approach;
I sketch an alternative, based on exponential rings, in future work.

Did the bet on automation pay off? I think so.
Our width-generic solvers prove more all-width problems than the unverified state of the art,
and our floating-point bitblaster, while slower than Bitwuzla,
offers end-to-end guarantees down to Lean's kernel in exchange.
What I learnt is a flavour of the bitter lesson: the only thing that scales is search,
and the best algorithms we have for SMT are the ones that reduce to SAT.
I ache a little at the beauty this leaves behind, interpolation above all,
but mechanizing these algorithms is within reach of today's proof assistants,
and foundational proof and push-button automation need not be at odds.
I hope these reductions lay the groundwork for verification efforts of far greater ambition.
