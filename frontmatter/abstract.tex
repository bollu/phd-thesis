Automated reasoning over finite domains---bitvectors of fixed width,
bitvectors of symbolic width, and floating-point numbers---underpins the
verification of compilers, hardware, and cryptography. SMT solvers decide such
theories efficiently, but they are large, unverified programs; interactive
theorem provers (ITPs) offer foundational guarantees, but have traditionally
lacked the automation that makes finite-domain reasoning practical. This
thesis narrows that gap: it contributes \emph{mechanized} decision procedures
for finite-domain theories, implemented and proved correct in the Lean proof
assistant, together with a verified rewriting framework that motivates and
consumes them.

We first formalize a core calculus for SSA-based compiler intermediate
representations, generic over the IR and covering MLIR-style regions. Our
mechanization provides a verified peephole rewriter, proof automation that
eliminates the abstraction overhead of the SSA calculus, and case studies
ranging from LLVM bitvector rewrites to fully homomorphic encryption.

Verifying such rewrites creates a demand for push-button bitvector
automation. We contribute mechanized decision procedures for
\emph{width-independent} bitvector predicates, viewing classical algorithms
through the lens of model checking: we mechanize $k$-induction, automata
reachability, emptiness, and minimization, and obtain scalable, certified
tactics that solve all of Hacker's Delight and large fractions of LLVM's
peephole test suite.

We then generalize from the mono-width to the multi-width setting. We prove that
any multi-width parametric bitvector formula reduces to an equisatisfiable
mono-width formula with only linear blowup, by encoding symbolic widths as
bitmasks. This reduction yields a bounded solver that replaces exponential
enumeration with a single fixed-width query, and, composed with our
automata-theoretic procedures, a sound and complete decision procedure for a
fragment strictly larger than the prior state of the art.

Finally, we extend mechanized bitblasting from bitvectors to floating-point
arithmetic. We mechanize the SMT-LIB theory of floating point, build verified
bitblasting circuits for its operations, and obtain the first verified
floating-point bitblaster, which runs within a constant geometric-mean
factor---about $33\times$---of the state-of-the-art unverified solver Bitwuzla,
while offering end-to-end correctness guarantees and supporting the small
formats used in machine-learning quantization.
