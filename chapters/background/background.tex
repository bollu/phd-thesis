\chapter{Background}
\label{ch:background}

\paragraph*{Attribution.} Parts of this chapter, in particular the account of
Lean's bitvector library and the \bvdecide{} tactic, are based on
\emph{Interactive Bitvector Reasoning using Verified
Bit-Blasting}~\citep{leanbv} (OOPSLA 2025), joint work led by Henrik Böving on
which I am a second author. That system is \emph{background} for this thesis:
the decision procedures of \cref{ch:single-width-bv,ch:multi-width-bv} and the
floating-point bitblaster of \cref{ch:floating-point} build on top of it.

\lettrine{B}{itvectors} are the lingua franca of low-level verification.
The pointer-free fragment of a programming language can be modelled by
bitvectors, matching the memory representation of integers, enums, and
structures; bitvectors likewise model integer operations in compiler IRs such
as LLVM-IR~\citep{zhao2012formalizing}, machine
code~\citep{armstrong2019isa,reid2016trustworthy}, combinational logic in
hardware~\citep{bourgeat2020essence}, and the substrate on which
floating-point arithmetic is defined~\citep{boldo2011flocq}. This chapter
recalls the tools this thesis assumes: SMT solving over the theory of
fixed-width bitvectors (\qfbv{}), bitblasting to SAT and certificate checking,
the Lean 4 proof assistant, and Lean's verified bitblaster \bvdecide{}.
